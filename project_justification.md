# Project Rationale and Assumptions

## Prioritisation logic
Stakeholders are prioritised using a combination of influence, impact on day-to-day adoption, and the consequences of misunderstanding the change.

- **Planners: highest adoption priority.** They use the process directly and may fear losing control. Involve them in testing, exception reviews, and feedback decisions.
- **Plant heads: high operational influence.** They translate planning decisions into production actions and can resolve operational constraints.
- **Board: high decision influence.** They need concise information about value, risk, governance, and decisions required.
- **Sales: high demand-information influence.** Customer and promotion context can explain demand changes that historical data alone may miss.
- **Procurement: high supply and inventory impact.** Forecasts affect purchasing timing, stock, lead times, and supplier commitments.
- **IT: high technical dependency.** Data quality, access, integrations, reliability, and support affect trust in the model.
- **Finance: financial control and benefit validation.** Finance checks whether inventory, waste, and service-level changes translate into measurable business outcomes.
- **Key yarn supplier: external dependency.** Supplier capacity and lead times matter, but forecast estimates must not be confused with confirmed purchase orders.

## Handling conflicting interests
- The board may want fast benefits, while planners need time to validate recommendations. Use a controlled pilot with explicit decision gates.
- Sales may prefer flexibility, while procurement needs stable lead-time signals. Share demand changes with confidence/exception context and retain normal purchase-order approval.
- Plant heads may prioritise utilisation, while finance may prioritise working capital. Track service, inventory, schedule stability, and cost together rather than optimising one metric alone.
- The supplier needs reliable notice, while the business needs responsiveness. Label forecasts as estimates and define when a change becomes a confirmed order.
- Staff may worry about job changes. Explain the purpose and scope honestly, provide a route for questions, and involve HR/employee representatives where relevant. Do not promise that no roles will change unless management has confirmed that commitment.

## Sentiment scoring
Use a consistent scale:
- **-2:** strongly resistant; refuses or actively blocks participation.
- **-1:** concerned/resistant; significant unresolved objections.
- **0:** neutral or undecided; willing to engage with questions.
- **+1:** supportive; participates and sees likely value.
- **+2:** champion; actively helps peers adopt the change.

Use evidence such as pulse survey results, meeting participation, issue themes, and observed actions. Do not treat attendance alone as proof of support.

## Trigger and intervention rules
- A drop of 1 or more points since the last check: contact the group representative within three working days, identify the cause, and agree an action.
- A score of -2: escalate to the project lead and sponsor; create a recovery plan within two working days.
- A high-severity forecast issue: acknowledge within one working day, validate data and operational impact, and communicate what is known, unknown, and next update time.
- A union or job-change concern: acknowledge respectfully, involve HR and the appropriate employee representatives, clarify verified facts, and avoid speculation or promises outside the approved change plan.
